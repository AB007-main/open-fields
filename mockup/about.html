About

Nobody keeps
a list of this

If you play, you already know the problem. There is a field somewhere near you with goals still standing. You don't know which one, whether the nets are on, or whether it turned into a swamp after Tuesday's rain. That knowledge lives in group texts and in the heads of the four people who go there most.

Open Fields is that knowledge, written down and put on a map.

How it stays accurate

A directory of fields goes stale in a year. Goals come down in November. A park gets renovated. A gate that was open all summer gets chained. So the listing is not the product, the last time a human confirmed it is.

  • Green means somebody confirmed the goals were up, recently.
  • Amber means the goals are seasonal, or nobody has checked in a while.
  • Gray means the field was submitted but never confirmed.

Anyone can flip a field's status with one tap after they play. That's the whole mechanism, and it's why the map is worth opening twice a week instead of once.

Conditions, not just goals

Whether a goal exists and whether the field is playable today are different questions. A turf field with two goals is useless if the gate is locked, and a grass field is useless the morning after a storm. Every field carries a live condition of playable, wet, or unplayable, reported by whoever was there last.

Who adds the fields

The first several dozen came from coaches who have driven to all of them. After that, anyone can submit one. Every submission is reviewed by a person before it appears, which keeps the map small and correct rather than large and wrong.

What this costs

Nothing, and there's no plan to charge for finding a field. The site carries a few ad slots and local sponsorships. If that ever conflicts with the map being accurate, the map wins.

Found a problem?

Wrong listing, missing field, or a bug in the site, tell us. If you manage a property and want it taken down, that form reaches a human.
